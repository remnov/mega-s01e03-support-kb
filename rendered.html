Shared month-first parser

The shared parser reads dates month first even for day-first tenants. App and email reports, and reports through booking-v2, reach this cause. The same applies to non-form reports from booking-v3 tenants. For Harbour Dental (en-GB, booking-v2), entering 07/04/2026 for 7 April produces 4 July. R-41200 records that reversal. Fix the parser to use the tenant’s specified date order, and reject ambiguous input when that order is unknown.

See the parser ticket. Its count follows the exported classifier, including the unresolved reports below.

Booking-v3 prints the wrong hint

For a day-first tenant, booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY, but the form reads the input in the tenant’s day-first order. Following the hint reverses month and day. This path requires channel form, booking-v3, and a day-first tenant. Copper Studio, Alder Physio, and Pale Harbour meet those tenant conditions. R-41230 is one example from Copper Studio. Use a hint consistent with the tenant’s parser and test the displayed hint and submitted date together. Fixing the shared parser alone does not correct this form’s instructions.

See the form-hint ticket.

These are separate fixes. Matching words in a subject do not establish a shared cause.

What the grouping does not prove

The classifier uses tenant configuration and channel; it does not inspect the report body. The desk notes flag R-41202, R-41209, R-41236, R-41344, and R-41620. All remain counted under parser by the exported rule. They are not proof of the parser defect: their bodies describe one-day offsets, an invite arriving before its booking, or different days in different cities. Keep them visible and investigate the original timestamp, tenant timezone, recipient timezone, expected date, and actual date before declaring their cause resolved. Do not invent a confirmed third defect from these symptoms or silently remove them from the count.

The raw export has overlapping pages. uniqueById counts each report ID once before filtering for #4471; adding raw row counts would overstate the number of reports. The table’s intake-tag breakdown also exposes disagreements between intake’s guess and the tenant-based cause.

When reports arrived and when the desk merged them

Reports began arriving in April. The large merge bar in early July is a filing event, not the start of the incident. Compare received with merged before calling a spike an increase in failures. New reports continue after the bulk merge; “known” does not mean fixed.

Replacement triage rule

  1. Read the body. Record the tenant, channel, expected date, actual date, locale, and form version.
  2. Look up the tenant’s settings; do not substitute the reporter’s browser locale or intake tag.
  3. For the date paths supported by this export, use causeOf and compare with sameCause.
  4. Merge only when the cause is known and matches. A matching subject alone is insufficient.
  5. If the tenant is missing, the cause is unknown, or the symptom contradicts the assigned cause, keep the report for investigation. Preserve its original ID and evidence when moving it.
  6. Count distinct report IDs. Link each cause to its own engineering ticket and check incoming reports again after a fix; do not mark the whole umbrella ticket resolved with one fix.

This trades the speed of subject-only matching for a cause the engineer can act on. It also prevents different subject lines from splitting the same cause into duplicate tickets.
